The sector in one view

  • One estimate puts the UAE automotive aftermarket at USD 7.2 billion in 2024, reaching USD 9.7 billion by 2030, about 5% a year. Ken Research
  • Demand follows the number of vehicles on the road and how hard they are used, so retention of older vehicles matters more than new-car sales in any one year.
  • Authorised workshops compete with independent chains, quick-service centres and mobile servicing once a vehicle is out of warranty.
  • For a distributor, aftersales is the steady profit line: labour margins are high and the customer base is already owned.

How the prototype maps to the job

First 90 days

  1. Days 1 to 30, baseline. Sit with each site and with Finance, Parts, Service, Warranty and Customer Experience. Write down every KPI definition in use and where each number comes from. Time the current month-end pack.
  2. Days 31 to 60, standardise. One scorecard, one template, one calendar. Agree definitions with the Aftersales Director and Finance. Hand reporting requirements to the ERP team with test cases.
  3. Days 61 to 90, automate and forecast. Pack assembled from the data layer, not by hand. First driver-based forecast: hours, rate, parts attach, retention. Action log reviewed monthly with owners.

Where I am not a direct fit: my planning and reporting experience is in B2B technology, not a dealership. The disciplines are the same; the workshop vocabulary I would learn on site in the first month.
